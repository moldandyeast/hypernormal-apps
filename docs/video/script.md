# hypernormal apps · the film, second cut

Storytelling rule, borrowed from Parker and Stone: every beat is forced by
the one before it, connected by *but* or *therefore*, never *and then*.
The connectors are visible: each act ends on a hard cut to one word,
full frame, half a second. "but." in grey. "therefore." in the foreground
color. The film is an argument that keeps interrupting itself.

The antagonist is the canonical frontend: the one perfect face, welded on.

No logo until the end. Start late, get out early. Silent; the typography
narrates. Solid panels are real output, dashed is illustration, # is
commentary. Real footage is the live installation, unstaged. About 100
seconds, 1080p30.

---

## beat 1 · the setup (0:00, ~8s) · motion

Cold open, no title. Two lines, then a small, lovingly drawn interface
appears in a dashed panel: buttons, a chart, tidy.

> you built an app.
> you designed its interface. it is perfect.

**CUT: but.**

## beat 2 · the contexts (~10s) · motion, fast

The perfect face is put through three contexts, smash cuts. Each context
line appears, and the pretty panel collapses into a different shape:

> your user is driving.        → the panel becomes one line: "blocked. we will talk later."
> your user cannot see.        → the panel becomes plain words, no layout at all.
> your user is not human.      → the panel becomes JSON.

**CUT: therefore.**

## beat 3 · the claim (~5s) · motion

> there is no perfect interface.
> there are only situations.

**CUT: but.**

## beat 4 · the villain (~7s) · motion

> every app you use ships one fixed interface.
> the one its maker chose.
> whatever your situation, you get the same compromise.

**CUT: therefore.**

## beat 5 · the separation (~12s) · motion, then REAL

> separate the interface from the app.

The split diagram: the dashed church drifts off screen; the solid box
stays and grows one line at a time:

> what remains is the app itself:
> its data, its actions, and its manual, at one web address.

REAL, tagged "live": the site's chapter 03 panel in close-up, the actual
charter JSON on screen.

**CUT: but.**

## beat 6 · the objection (~14s) · motion, then REAL

> an app with no interface sounds useless.
> so anyone may build one. here are five.

REAL: the two-window ripple. One click, both windows flip, seq identical,
"2 here". Then the lens cuts, fast, 1.5 seconds each, path tags on:
timeline, quilt, swatch tapped, console.

> this is one face. this is another. and another. and another.
> none of them is the app.

**CUT: but.**

## beat 7 · the agent (~10s) · motion + REAL

> your ai agent needs no interface at all.

The getTools line types on; the tool calls fire; then REAL: the console
lens, a verb typed by hand, the result and the state stream arriving.

> the app's actions register as browser tools (webmcp) the moment the page opens.
> nothing installed. no account. no sdk.

**CUT: but.**

## beat 8 · the trust problem (~9s) · motion

> anyone can act on this app.
> so why trust it?

The verb's code types on, verbatim, three lines.

> every action's code is public. read it before you trust it.

**CUT: but.**

## beat 9 · the receipts (~11s) · motion + REAL

> maybe this is all a mockup.
> it is not. it has been running the whole time.

REAL: the quilt, hundreds of stitches. Overlaid:

> 200 changes by strangers, each recorded once.
> every midnight the app resets itself. nobody is there when it happens.

**CUT: therefore.**

## beat 10 · the close (~12s) · motion

The title, arriving only now, typed with the cursor block:

> hypernormal apps ▮
> durable faceless apps. a white paper that runs.

> github.com/moldandyeast/hypernormal-apps · hypernormal.moldandyeast.com

Last line, live accent, cursor blinking:

> an empty URL is waiting ▮

## why this is not the first cut

The first cut walked the site top to bottom: and then, and then, and then.
This one has a want (the perfect face), a collapse (contexts), a villain
(the welded-on frontend), a turn (the separation), three objections raised
and answered (useless, agent, trust), a doubter silenced with receipts, and
only then the name. Every real clip now lands as evidence inside an
argument instead of scenery inside a tour.

## production notes

- Connector cards: "but." (grey) and "therefore." (foreground), 0.6s each,
  hard cuts, no easing.
- New captures needed: chapter 03 charter panel close-up; a tighter quilt.
  The ripple, lens, and console footage from the first shoot is reusable.
- Beats 1 to 4 are new motion. Beats 5 to 10 recut existing material with
  new timing: faster, nothing on screen longer than it needs to be read
  once.

## language rule (third pass)

No internal vocabulary before the film has earned it: interface, not face;
the manual, not charter; actions, not verbs; data, not state; one web
address, not url-of-record. Every real clip's overlay says what you are
seeing, not what we call it.
